Introduction

In celebration of World Tourism Day and the United Nations Celebration, STI College Marikina Senior High School and Tertiary Departments will hold a special event. This activity aims to highlight the significance of global awareness, cultural appreciation, and the role of tourism in promoting unity and development across nations.

Each section will be divided into groups and supervised by their assigned teachers. Every group will create a poster and an educational short video that creatively showcase the beauty, richness, and unique identity of different countries around the world. Through this event, students will not only learn about various cultures but also develop a deeper sense of respect, inclusivity, and global citizenship.

All students are required to participate by both presenting and viewing the exhibits and screenings in designated showing rooms, ensuring a shared learning experience that celebrates diversity and creativity.

Learning Objectives

By the end of the activity, the students are expected to:

  • Demonstrate understanding of global diversity by showcasing the cultural, historical, and natural richness of different nations through creative outputs.
  • Enhance collaboration and communication skills through group planning, production, and presentation of their posters and educational shorts.
  • Foster appreciation and respect for cultural differences by actively engaging in viewing sessions and reflecting on the importance of unity in diversity.

Poster Making Competition

"A Window to the World"

Participants will create a digital poster that serves as a visual invitation to their assigned country, highlighting its unique culture, landmarks, and contributions to the world.

  1. The poster must be digital and submitted in a high-resolution format (JPEG or PNG).
  2. Dimensions should be standard poster size (e.g., 18x24 inches) in portrait orientation.
  3. The design must include the name of the country and a compelling tagline.
  4. It should visually represent key aspects of the country's culture, heritage, or natural beauty.
  5. All visual elements (images, graphics) must be original or used with proper attribution (royalty-free).
  6. The poster should be informative, engaging, and artistically appealing.

Short Video Competition

"Voices of the Globe"

This competition challenges groups to produce a short educational video (e.g., a mini-documentary or travelogue) that tells a compelling story about their assigned country.

  1. The video must be between 2 to 3 minutes in length.
  2. The format should be MP4 with a minimum resolution of 720p.
  3. The content should be educational, focusing on a specific cultural aspect, historical event, or unique feature of the country.
  4. Narration or subtitles in English must be included.
  5. All media used must be original or properly credited (royalty-free music, footage).
  6. The video must end with a concluding message that connects the country's story to a global theme (e.g., unity, preservation, innovation).

Headdress Design Competition

"A Bridge to Tomorrow: Weaving SDGs into Cultural Attire"

This competition challenges participants to create a headdress that represents a specific country and one of the UN's Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The design must blend traditional attire with symbols of the chosen SDG, creating a "Bridge to Tomorrow."

  1. Each group will be assigned a country and a Sustainable Development Goal (SDG).
  2. The headdress must be based on a traditional or culturally significant attire from the assigned country.
  3. The design must visibly and creatively incorporate elements representing the assigned SDG (e.g., colors, symbols, patterns).
  4. A significant portion of the headdress must be made from sustainable, recycled, or upcycled materials. Groups are heavily encouraged to use repurposed materials (e.g., plastic bottles, newspapers, bottle caps) and non-toxic dyes.
  5. The headdress should be a wearable piece of art, not just a static display.
  6. A brief written explanation (150-200 words) must accompany the entry, detailing the cultural inspiration, SDG integration, and materials used.
  7. The headdress must accurately reflect the cultural identity it represents, avoiding stereotypes and cultural appropriation. Proper research is crucial.
  8. The final piece must demonstrate quality construction, durability, and neat finishing.
  9. The design should creatively adapt the traditional attire to convey the SDG message without undermining its cultural roots.
